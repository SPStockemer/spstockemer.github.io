The October Radio Hour

The October Radio Hour is a Halloween horror anthology presented as a single 1940s radio broadcast that only airs during the month of October.

Hosted by the smooth, unseen voice of Graham Reaper, each broadcast introduces a new standalone tale of supernatural horror. Ride a bus that carries the dead to their final destination. Descend into a forgotten furnace level where Halloween never ended. Answer a hotel wake-up call from your future self. Watch candles in darkened windows guide something home that was never meant to return. Cross a river where the dead wait for the last ferry before dawn, and meet a stranger who arrives just a little too early for the final Halloween supper.

Written as prose with the pace and atmosphere of classic radio theater, these ten stories build toward a chilling conclusion as the broadcast itself begins to change.

Turn down the lights. Turn up the volume.
The October Radio Hour is about to begin.

The Dead Order

When death comes, most people expect judgment.
What they receive is paperwork.

A man awakens in a silent ward where every answer he gives is marked INCORRECT. A safety coordinator discovers her afterlife has already been claimed by something waiting beyond the intake desk. Across a vast bureaucracy that governs the dead, files move, claims are filed, identities are corrected, and every soul is processed according to rules no living person was ever meant to understand.

Within the hidden machinery of the Dead Order, clerks record the newly departed, auditors reconcile contradictions, and entire departments oversee what comes after the final breath. Some souls are misplaced. Some are disputed. Some discover that death is not an ending at all, but the beginning of a system far older and far larger than humanity ever imagined.

The Dead Order is a collection of ten interconnected horror stories exploring the strange administration of the afterlife, where every death opens a file, every file demands resolution, and the rules are never explained to those forced to obey them.

The Dust & The Damned

Out on the edges of nowhere, the rules don’t hold.

The land remembers what was buried in it.
The wind carries things that don’t belong to the living.
And some roads don’t lead anywhere… they just take.

In this collection of dark, atmospheric horror, each story drags you deeper into places where something has already gone wrong—and isn’t finished yet.

No heroes.
No clean endings.
Just the slow realization that once you’ve stepped into it… you were never getting out.

If you like your horror quiet, relentless, and just a little too close to real, The Dust & The Damned is waiting.

Lanterns in the Fog

There are places where something doesn’t stay where it belongs.

A voice comes through that shouldn’t exist.
A signal carries something it wasn’t meant to.
A room holds onto what happened inside it… and refuses to let it go.

Most people ignore it.

In these stories, the fog is not just something you walk through—it’s where reality begins to break its boundaries. The past doesn’t stay buried. Systems don’t shut down. Messages don’t stop once they’ve been sent.

They continue.

A late-night broadcast picks up something that already happened.
A forgotten object plays back more than sound.
A place remembers what was done there—and answers for it.

And the lanterns?

They don’t guide you out.

They illuminate what’s bleeding through—moment by moment—until the line between what was, what is, and what should never return collapses completely.

Because this isn’t random.

It’s structured.
It’s persistent.
And once it reaches you… it doesn’t let go.

Each story moves deeper into a world where events echo long after they end, where reality reshapes itself without warning, and where the truth arrives too late to escape it.

By the time you understand what you’re hearing…

It’s already happening to you.

Every light reveals what the fog tries to hide.

Midnight Frights

Tonight’s features… don’t fade when the lights come up.

Midnight Frights is a collection of chilling tales inspired by the golden age of 1950s horror—where creatures lurk in the dark, experiments go wrong, and something always waits just beyond the edge of reason.

Each story plays like a lost late-night feature: strange, atmospheric, and creeping with dread. Small towns hide terrible secrets. The unknown refuses to stay buried. And the line between fiction and reality begins to slip.

But these aren’t just stories meant to entertain.
They linger.
They follow.
And when the final page turns, something remains.
Because not every nightmare ends when the lights come up.

Step into the dark.

And stay through the end… if you can.

The October Tales

The October Tales is a collection of eerie short stories rooted in the magic—and menace—of the Halloween season.

Across quiet towns and forgotten places, something waits just beyond the edge of the familiar. Shadows move where they shouldn’t. Old houses remember too much. And in some places, the darkness isn’t empty… it’s watching.

From cursed homes and restless spirits to deeper, more dangerous forces lurking behind the veil of autumn, each story pulls you further into a world where October isn’t just a season—it’s a gateway.

These are tales of nostalgia twisted into dread, where every flicker of candlelight holds back something that would rather see you in the dark.

The nights grow longer. October doesn’t end when the calendar does.

About the Author

S.P. Stockemer writes horror rooted in atmosphere, memory, and the unsettling possibility that the familiar world holds more secrets than we realize. His stories explore the places where ordinary life begins to unravel, where forgotten histories refuse to remain buried, and where the most disturbing discoveries often arrive quietly.

His work draws on a fascination with the strange and overlooked: old cemeteries, abandoned places, forgotten roads, local legends, and the lingering character of places that have outlived their original purpose. For Stockemer, a setting is rarely just a backdrop. It is often as important to a story as the people who inhabit it.

Nostalgia also plays a significant role in his fiction. From the eerie charm of vintage Halloween traditions to the theatrical suspense of classic radio horror and the creature features of an earlier era, his stories frequently revisit familiar elements of the past and find something unexpected waiting beneath them.

Across collections such as The October Tales, Midnight Frights, Lanterns in the Fog, The Dust & The Damned, The Dead Order, and The October Radio Hour, Stockemer explores different corners of the horror genre. Some stories embrace the supernatural. Others find their terror in human behavior, unexplained circumstances, or the gradual realization that something is terribly wrong. What connects them is an emphasis on storytelling, atmosphere, and the lingering questions that remain after the final page.

Beyond writing, Stockemer is a longtime musician and performer, with a background that has kept storytelling at the center of his creative life. He is also an avid photographer, drawn particularly to historic cemeteries, unusual locations, and the forgotten details of the American landscape.

Based in Texas, S.P. Stockemer continues to develop new collections and explore fresh territory in horror fiction, guided by a simple fascination: the world is full of stories, and some of the most interesting ones are hiding in its darkest corners.
